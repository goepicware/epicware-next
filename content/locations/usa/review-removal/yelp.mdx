## Pain

Yelp is the one platform where "just ask them to take it down" almost never works — and most businesses don't find that out until they've already tried and failed.

Yelp won't remove a review because it's negative, and its automated recommendation software quietly filters reviews it doesn't trust into a separate section that doesn't even count toward your star rating — which means a business can be fighting the wrong problem entirely, trying to get a visible 1-star review removed when the real issue is genuine reviews landing in "Not Recommended" instead.

Meanwhile, a genuinely fake review — one that breaks Yelp's actual content guidelines — sits there collecting views, because clicking "report" with no evidence behind it rarely moves a moderator to act.

## FAQ

### What's the actual difference between a review Yelp removes and one it marks "Not Recommended"?

A removed review is gone — Yelp's moderation team confirmed it broke the content guidelines (a fake review, harassment, a conflict of interest) and took it down. A "Not Recommended" review is still live and viewable through a link at the bottom of the page; Yelp's software just doesn't trust it enough yet to count it toward your star rating. They need two different responses: a removal report for the first, patience or more genuine reviews for the second.

### Can Yelp staff manually move a review out of "Not Recommended" if I ask nicely?

No — Yelp states explicitly that no employee can override the recommendation software, specifically to avoid any appearance that advertising or a direct ask influences which reviews count. That's actually useful to know: don't waste time asking support to intervene on a "Not Recommended" review; the lever that works is building the reviewer's trust signals over time, or filing a removal report if the review itself breaks a content rule rather than just failing the trust bar.

### Does advertising with Yelp get my negative reviews suppressed?

No, and Yelp is explicit that advertising has no connection to how reviews are treated. If a competitor or reviewer implies that paying Yelp "fixes" reviews, that's not how the system works — removal only happens through an actual content-guideline breach.

### What is a Yelp Consumer Alert, and could my business get one by mistake?

A Consumer Alert is a public warning Yelp places on a business page when it has evidence of review manipulation — paid reviews, coordinated posting, or legal threats against a reviewer, among other triggers — and Yelp says it investigates before placing one. A legitimate business that isn't manipulating reviews shouldn't trigger one; if you believe an alert was placed in error, that's a separate dispute from a standard review removal and needs its own evidence.

### My competitor seems to be posting fake 5-star reviews for themselves — can I report that to Yelp?

Yes, and Yelp's own enforcement data shows this is exactly the kind of activity it actively investigates — its 2025 report cites nearly 2,000 accounts closed specifically for review-ring activity. We document the pattern (shared posting timing, reviewer accounts with no other history, language similarities) and route it as the fake-engagement or conflict-of-interest issue it actually is.

### How long does a Yelp removal decision take once filed?

It depends on Yelp's queue and whether the case needs escalation, typically running from days to a few weeks. We follow up at every stage and tell you the outcome either way rather than leaving you to guess.
