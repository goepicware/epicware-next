## Pain

In a market as saturated as Los Angeles, a handful of fake 1-star reviews doesn't just dent your rating — it can flip which business shows up above yours in the exact same search.

When a competitor, a disgruntled ex-employee, or a bot drops a fabricated review on your Google or Yelp profile, the damage compounds fast in a category where a dozen near-identical options are one scroll away. The fake review doesn't need to convince everyone. It just needs to tip the next click.

Most LA owners click "report" once, hear nothing back from the platform, and let it go. The review stays exactly where it landed. The next search goes to whoever's left standing at 4.6 stars instead of 4.3.

The FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465) took effect on 21 October 2024 and lets the FTC seek civil penalties for fake reviews. The Consumer Review Fairness Act voids non-disparagement clauses in consumer form contracts, so a business cannot contract its way out of honest negative reviews. California Civil Code Section 1670.8 — signed into law in 2014 as AB 2365, popularly called the "Yelp bill" — bars consumer contracts from waiving a customer's right to post an honest review, and makes it unlawful to threaten or penalize a customer for one, predating the federal Consumer Review Fairness Act by about two years. We only report a review because it breaks a platform's actual content policy, never because a star rating is inconvenient.

In its own April 2026 transparency update covering 2025, Google reported blocking or removing more than 292 million policy-violating reviews, restricting over 782,000 accounts, and removing more than 13 million fake Business Profiles — proof that most fake reviews never survive long enough to need a removal report at all. The ones that do are usually built specifically to look like normal activity, which is exactly the kind of case we document.

A genuinely bad but honest review we leave exactly where it is, and help you answer it well instead — a specific, professional reply often wins back more trust than a deletion would.

## FAQ

### California was the first state to ban non-disparagement clauses — does that mean I can't be sued for leaving a bad review here?

That's actually the flip side of what we do: California's 2014 law protects customers who leave honest reviews from being threatened or penalized for it. It doesn't protect a business from a fake review — if anything, it reinforces the same principle our removal work follows, that the fix for a bad review is honesty, not intimidation on either side.

### LA has a huge number of competing businesses in every category — does that make fake-review attacks more common here?

Dense, highly competitive categories (restaurants, salons, contractors, med spas) are where we see the most coordinated fake-review activity, because one listing's rating directly steers traffic to the next one down the search results. A pattern across several competitors' profiles, or several reviews posted from accounts with no other history, is one of the stronger signals we document.

### My business has multiple locations across LA County — do you handle all of them under one audit?

Yes. We scope the review audit per Google Business Profile and Yelp page, but a multi-location business gets one coordinated engagement rather than a separate, disconnected process for every address.

### Does Google's own 2025 enforcement data mean most fake reviews already get caught automatically?

Most get caught before they're ever seen, by Google's own account — but "most" still leaves plenty that slip through on an individual profile, especially a targeted attack built to look like normal activity. Automated detection at Google's scale and a specific, evidence-backed report on your profile are solving different problems; we do the second one.

### What counts as a "policy-violating" review versus one that's just negative?

Google's own enforcement categories include fake engagement (not from a real experience, paid or incentivized), rating manipulation (coordinated posting patterns, conflict-of-interest reviews) and impersonation — all distinct from a customer's honest, if harsh, account of a real visit. We check each review against those actual categories before we file anything.

### How long before I see a decision on a filed Los Angeles review?

Typically days to a few weeks for an initial decision, longer if it's appealed. We follow up at every stage and report the outcome either way, rather than leaving you to refresh the page.
