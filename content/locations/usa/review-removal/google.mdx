## Pain

Google's enforcement machine blocks hundreds of millions of fake reviews a year — and your specific one can still be sitting at the top of your profile right now, because that scale is built to catch patterns across the whole platform, not necessarily the one review aimed at you.

A vague "report" click, with no policy category named and no evidence attached, usually gets auto-rejected or simply ignored. The review stays. The star average stays dragged down. The next searcher sees it before they see anything you've actually done right.

Most owners don't realize Google sorts violations into specific categories — Fake Engagement, Rating Manipulation, Impersonation, Misrepresentation — and that naming the right one, with matching proof, is most of what separates a report that works from one that doesn't.

## FAQ

### Which exact Google policy category does a fake review usually fall under?

Most fake reviews we file against fall under Fake Engagement (not based on a real experience, or paid/incentivized) or Rating Manipulation (a conflict-of-interest reviewer, or an unusual burst of posting activity designed to shift the rating). Naming the specific category in a filing, with the matching evidence, is a big part of why a report gets acted on instead of auto-rejected.

### Google says 292 million reviews were blocked or removed last year — does that mean fake reviews rarely make it onto an individual profile?

Most of that volume is caught automatically before a review is ever published, by Google's own account — but automated filtering at that scale is still built to catch patterns, not every individually crafted fake review on one specific profile. A targeted attack on your listing is exactly the kind of case that needs a direct, evidence-backed report rather than relying on Google's filters to catch it.

### Can one bad review restrict my whole Google Business Profile, or even my account?

A single review doesn't trigger that — Google's own policy describes restriction as a response to a repeated pattern of violations, and a restricted Google account suspends every Business Profile tied to it, not just one. That's actually a reason to get genuinely fake reviews removed quickly: letting a pattern of fake engagement accumulate on your profile (even reviews posted by a competitor, not you) raises the account-level risk unnecessarily.

### Does responding publicly to a fake review hurt my case for getting it removed?

Not inherently, but a reply that argues the facts of the review can make it look more like a genuine dispute than a policy violation to a moderator skimming the case. We typically hold off on a public reply to a review we're actively filing against, and draft a response only for the reviews we're not pursuing removal on.

### What if the review mentions a real visit but makes up details, like a wrong order or a manager who doesn't work there?

That's a misrepresentation or off-topic case rather than a clean fake-engagement one, and it needs different evidence — staffing records, order history, or similar specifics that contradict the review's claims. We tell you honestly when a review is a weaker case before you pay for it.

### How long does a Google review decision usually take once filed?

It varies with Google's queue and whether appeal is needed, typically running from days to a few weeks. We follow up at every stage and report the outcome either way.
