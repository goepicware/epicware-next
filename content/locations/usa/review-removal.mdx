### What we won't do (and why that protects you)

The FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465) took effect on 21 October 2024 and lets the FTC seek civil penalties for fake reviews. The same rule bans buying positive or negative reviews and using groundless legal threats or intimidation to suppress negative reviews. The Consumer Review Fairness Act voids non-disparagement clauses in consumer form contracts, so a business cannot contract its way out of honest negative reviews.

So we never:

- report a genuine review just because it's negative,
- offer reviewers anything to edit or delete a review,
- threaten legal action over honest opinions.

Every report we file is one you could show a regulator.

## FAQ

### Can you remove any negative Google review?

No, and anyone who promises that is either lying or breaking Google's rules. Google removes reviews that violate its content policy, such as spam, fake engagement, conflicts of interest, off-topic or offensive content. A genuine bad experience, described honestly, stays up. We tell you which category each review falls into before you pay.

### How is a Yelp review different from a Google review?

Yelp won't remove a review simply because it's negative, and its recommendation software can hide reviews from the main rating without deleting them. ### Is it legal to get reviews removed in the US?

Yes, when the review breaks platform rules. What the FTC targets is the opposite: buying reviews, faking them, or suppressing honest ones through threats or gag clauses. Reporting a genuinely fake review is exactly what the platforms ask businesses to do.
